10:T4ca,During World War II (1939-1945), the US and Soviet Union were allies — they fought together against Nazi Germany. But they had very different ideas about how the world should be organised after the war ended.

The United States believed in capitalism — an economic system where people and businesses own property, make their own money, and compete in a free market. Think of it like a school canteen where different students run different food stalls, competing to sell the best lunch. The US also believed in democracy — where people vote to choose their leaders.

The Soviet Union believed in communism — an economic system where the government owns most property and businesses, and shares wealth equally among all citizens. Imagine if your school canteen was run by the government, which decided what food to serve and gave everyone the same meal. The Soviet Union was also a dictatorship — one person (the leader) made most decisions without asking people to vote.

After WWII, the Soviet Union took control of countries in Eastern Europe. The US worried communism would spread everywhere. The Soviet Union worried the US would try to destroy their communist system.
