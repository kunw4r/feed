10:T64d,## Historical Context
AUKUS was announced in September 2021 as a response to China's military modernisation. It replaced France's Attack-class submarine contract (which cost $90 billion and was cancelled). Nuclear submarines are considered essential for modern navies because they're faster, quieter, and can stay submerged longer than conventional submarines. Australia has never built nuclear submarines before, making this an unprecedented industrial undertaking.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Government/Defence view**: China's military spending has tripled in 15 years; nuclear submarines are non-negotiable for deterrence and protecting Australia's interests.
**Economic critics**: $368 billion could fund 100,000 new teachers, rebuild aged care, or address housing crisis. The cost-per-submarine ($46 billion) is extraordinarily high.
**Industry view**: The program will create 20,000+ jobs and establish Australia as a high-tech manufacturing hub.
**Geopolitical view**: AUKUS strengthens US-Australia alliance and signals commitment to Indo-Pacific stability.

## Future Implications
If submarines arrive in 2038, they'll be cutting-edge but Australia will have a capability gap until then. China's military will likely be even more advanced by 2038. The program's success depends on maintaining political consensus across decades and managing industrial complexity. Cost overruns could force cuts to other defence capabilities.
