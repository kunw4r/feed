In February 2026, homicide rates have reached their highest levels in years, with cartels using increasingly sophisticated weapons and tactics. The Mexican government has deployed military forces to major cities, but critics argue the strategy isn't working because cartels have deeper resources and local corruption runs too deep.

Why this matters: Drug cartels don't just affect Mexico. They smuggle cocaine, fentanyl, and methamphetamine into North America. The violence destabilises the region, forces migration northward, and creates humanitarian crises. When cartels control territory, they also control who lives there—essentially running parallel governments.

The root cause is demand. Americans consume massive quantities of illegal drugs, creating enormous profits for cartels. Mexico sits between South American drug producers and North American consumers, making it the battleground. The US has provided military aid and training, but without addressing addiction and demand at home, the cycle continues.
