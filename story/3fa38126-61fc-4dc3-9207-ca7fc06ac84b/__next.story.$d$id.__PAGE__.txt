3:T637,## Historical Context
Labour won a landslide in 2020 under Jacinda Ardern, but she resigned in January 2023 due to burnout. Her successor, Chris Hipkins, inherited an economy struggling with post-pandemic inflation. New Zealand's Reserve Bank raised interest rates aggressively (to 5.5%) to combat inflation, making mortgages and loans more expensive. This created a political backlash as younger voters and first-home buyers faced unaffordable housing.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Labour view**: Global inflation was caused by pandemic supply chains and energy shocks; they managed better than many countries.
**Voters' view**: Inflation destroyed purchasing power; wages didn't keep up; government failed to deliver promised housing solutions.
**National Party view**: Labour's spending was excessive; lower taxes and less regulation will restore economic growth.
**Economists' view**: New Zealand faces structural challenges (housing shortage, productivity) that no single government can solve quickly.

## Future Implications
A National-led government will likely cut government spending, reduce taxes, and deregulate. This could slow inflation but might worsen inequality. Trade relationships with Australia (NZ's largest trading partner) may shift depending on coalition partners' priorities. Regional climate initiatives might be deprioritised if coalition includes climate-sceptical parties.
