3:T4f6,China's real estate sector is in serious trouble. Evergrande, one of the world's largest property developers, has defaulted on billions of dollars in debt, joining other major Chinese builders in financial collapse. This matters because property development has been the engine of China's economy for decades—it employs millions and generates massive tax revenue for the government.

The crisis started when the Chinese government cracked down on excessive borrowing by developers in 2020. Companies had borrowed heavily to build apartments, but when the government tightened lending rules, they couldn't refinance their debts. Now, thousands of apartment buildings sit half-finished, and Chinese citizens who paid for homes years ago are still waiting. This has destroyed consumer confidence and savings.

Why this affects Australia: Chinese economic slowdown means less demand for Australian iron ore, coal, and agricultural exports. This threatens Australian jobs and government revenue. Additionally, Chinese investors have pulled money out of Australian real estate, affecting property markets in Sydney and Melbourne. The Chinese government is trying stimulus measures, but experts doubt they'll work without fundamental reforms to how the economy is structured.4:T61b,## Historical Context
China's property boom began in the 1990s when the government privatised housing. Developers borrowed aggressively, and property became both a home and an investment vehicle for Chinese citizens (since stock markets were seen as risky). By 2020, property represented 30% of GDP and 60% of household wealth. The government's 2020 crackdown on "three red lines" (debt-to-equity ratios, debt-to-assets, and cash-to-debt) was meant to prevent a bubble, but it triggered defaults instead.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Government view**: The crackdown was necessary to prevent unsustainable debt. Officials argue stimulus and restructuring will stabilise the sector.

**Developer perspective**: Lending restrictions were too sudden and severe, making it impossible to service existing debt.

**Citizen perspective**: Millions have lost life savings in unfinished apartments and lost faith in the system.

**International investors**: China's slowdown reduces global growth and creates financial contagion risks.

## Future Implications
China may need a massive government bailout (potentially 10-15% of GDP). This could trigger inflation, currency weakness, or capital controls. The property sector may shrink permanently, forcing economic restructuring toward consumption and services rather than investment and exports.
