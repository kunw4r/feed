Germany, Poland, and the Baltic states are leading the surge, with Germany alone doubling its defence budget to 3% of GDP (government spending). This is the largest military investment in Europe since the 1980s Cold War era.

Why this matters: For decades, European nations relied on the US for military protection through NATO. Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine shocked Europe into realising it couldn't depend solely on America—especially with unpredictable US politics. Countries are now buying fighter jets, tanks, air defence systems, and cyber warfare capabilities. This spending creates jobs in defence manufacturing but also raises tensions with Russia, which views NATO expansion as a threat.

Australia is watching closely because European military strength affects global stability. During the Cold War (1947-1991), Europe was heavily militarised with NATO and Soviet forces facing off. After the Soviet Union collapsed, European defence spending dropped dramatically—by 2020, most EU nations spent only 1-2% of GDP on defence. Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 reversed this trend. NATO's founding principle is collective defence: an attack on one member is an attack on all. This principle is now being tested and taken seriously.

## Multiple Perspectives
**European governments**: Russia is an existential threat; military strength is necessary for survival. Spending on defence is an investment in peace through deterrence.

**Russian perspective**: NATO's military buildup is aggressive expansion encircling Russia. Moscow claims it's forced to militarise in response, creating an arms race.

**US perspective**: Welcomes European rearmament as it reduces American burden. However, some US politicians worry Europe might develop independent military capacity and reduce NATO dependence.

**Peace advocates**: Argue military spending diverts resources from climate change, healthcare, and poverty reduction. Worry that arms races increase accident risks and miscalculation.

**Defence industry**: Profits from increased orders for weapons, technology, and military services.

## Future Implications
If spending continues: Europe becomes militarily independent, potentially reducing NATO reliance on the US. This could reshape global power dynamics. If Russia escalates in response, Europe faces an arms race. Alternatively, credible military strength might deter Russian aggression, making conflict less likely.

- Whether spending targets are actually met (governments often announce plans but underfund them)
