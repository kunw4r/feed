3:T709,## Historical Context
The Federal Reserve raised rates aggressively from 2022-2023 to combat inflation that reached 9%. Rates have been gradually cut since, but inflation remains sticky (slow to fall). Trump previously criticised the Fed during his first presidency, and the Fed's independence has always been politically contentious.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Federal Reserve**: Inflation is still above target. Tariffs will increase prices further. Cutting rates prematurely risks re-igniting inflation and undermining credibility. The Fed must remain independent from political pressure.

**Trump Administration**: The economy needs lower rates to boost growth and stock markets. The Fed is being too restrictive. Tariffs will increase competition and actually reduce prices over time.

**Markets/Investors**: Lower rates would boost asset prices (stocks, real estate) but risk inflation. Higher rates protect savings but slow growth. Uncertainty about Fed independence creates volatility.

**Economists**: Most support the Fed's cautious approach. Tariffs are inflationary; cutting rates would worsen inflation. Fed independence is crucial for long-term economic stability.

## Future Implications
If the Fed maintains higher rates: slower economic growth, potential recession, but stable inflation. If it cuts rates under political pressure: risk of inflation returning, currency weakness, but stronger short-term growth. The outcome depends on whether tariffs actually increase inflation significantly.

## What to Watch
- Inflation data over coming months (will tariffs increase prices?)
- Stock market volatility and investor confidence
- Trump's continued criticism of the Fed and potential attempts to replace Fed leadership
