10:T6be,The Sahel region—a band of countries stretching across West Africa from Mauritania to Chad—is experiencing unprecedented instability, with five military coups in the past three years and extremist groups like ISIS and Al-Qaeda expanding their control. Mali, Burkina Faso, and Niger have all experienced coups since 2020, with military leaders seizing power and promising to fight terrorism but instead consolidating authoritarian control. The coups have destabilised the region, driven out international peacekeeping forces, and created power vacuums that extremist groups exploit to recruit fighters and establish bases.

The Sahel is crucial to global security because it sits between North Africa and sub-Saharan Africa, and extremist groups based there can launch attacks across the region and beyond. The instability has displaced 4 million people and created a humanitarian crisis comparable to Syria. Terrorist groups are using the chaos to expand their territorial control, establish training camps, and plan attacks on neighbouring countries and Europe. France, which had 5,000 troops in Mali fighting extremists, withdrew in 2022 after the military coup, leaving a security vacuum.

The root causes are complex: poverty, climate change reducing farmland, weak governance, and historical grievances between ethnic groups. Military coups promise quick solutions but actually worsen instability by alienating populations and driving them toward extremist groups. Regional organisations like ECOWAS (Economic Community of West African States) have imposed sanctions on coup leaders, but enforcement is weak.
