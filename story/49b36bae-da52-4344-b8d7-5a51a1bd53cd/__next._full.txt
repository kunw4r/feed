11:T633,The UN's Article 6 carbon trading mechanism—a system designed to let countries and companies buy and sell carbon credits to meet climate targets—is struggling after the US, India, and several EU nations announced they're pulling out in February 2026. Here's how it works: countries commit to reducing emissions by a certain amount. If they can't, they buy 'carbon credits' from other countries that have reduced emissions MORE than required. One credit = one tonne of CO2 not released. The problem? The system has been plagued by fraud. Companies in developing nations have been selling fake credits—claiming they prevented emissions that never actually happened. This undermines the entire mechanism because wealthy nations end up paying for emissions reductions that don't exist, meaning global emissions keep rising despite the trading.

Why this matters: The UN relies on member countries' contributions (called 'assessed contributions') to fund its operations—Australia pays about $50 million annually. When major economies lose faith in UN climate systems, they're less likely to fund them or cooperate on future agreements. This weakens the UN's ability to coordinate global climate action. The withdrawal also signals that countries are increasingly pursuing their own climate strategies rather than trusting international frameworks.

The UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) was established in 1992 at the Rio Earth Summit. Article 6 of the Paris Agreement (2015) created the carbon credit mechanism as a 'market-based solution'—the idea being that capitalism could solve climate change by making emissions reductions profitable. The system assumes that countries and companies will be honest about their emissions reductions. This assumption has proven catastrophically wrong.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Wealthy nations' view**: Carbon credits should only be valid if independently verified. They're angry they've been defrauded and want stricter oversight.

**Developing nations' view**: They argue that wealthy countries created this problem by not funding climate action directly. They say the West is using fraud as an excuse to avoid paying what they owe for historical emissions.

**Environmental groups**: They argue the entire carbon credit system is a distraction—it allows polluters to keep polluting by buying offsets instead of actually reducing emissions.

**Financial markets**: Investment firms have made billions trading carbon credits. They're lobbying for the system to continue with reforms rather than be abandoned.

## Future Implications
If Article 6 collapses, the Paris Agreement loses a key mechanism for achieving its 1.5°C target. This could trigger a cascade of countries abandoning climate commitments. Alternatively, it could force a shift toward direct government funding of climate action rather than market mechanisms.
