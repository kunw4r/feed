Tariffs have been central to Trump's economic policy since his first presidency (2017-2021). The 2018-2019 US-China trade war saw escalating tariffs that disrupted global supply chains and contributed to economic slowdown. Trump argues tariffs protect American workers and manufacturing; critics say they increase inflation and reduce consumer purchasing power.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Trump Administration**: Tariffs are necessary to address unfair Chinese trade practices, intellectual property theft, and to rebuild American manufacturing capacity. They're a negotiating tool to force better trade deals.

**Economists/Business Groups**: Tariffs increase costs for American consumers and businesses, reduce competitiveness, and invite retaliation. They're an inefficient way to address trade imbalances.

**China/EU**: These tariffs violate WTO rules and are protectionist measures that harm global trade. They'll respond with their own tariffs.

**Developing Nations**: Trade wars between superpowers create uncertainty that affects smaller economies' ability to plan investments and trade.

## Future Implications
If tariffs remain, expect: higher consumer prices (especially electronics, clothing, cars), slower global GDP growth, potential recession, supply chain reorganisation away from China, and increased geopolitical tension. Negotiations may lead to modified tariffs or exemptions.
