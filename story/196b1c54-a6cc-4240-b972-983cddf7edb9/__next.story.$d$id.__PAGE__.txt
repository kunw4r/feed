The Arctic was historically frozen and remote, so geopolitically irrelevant. Climate change is melting sea ice, opening shipping routes and exposing mineral wealth. The 1982 UN Convention on the Law of the Sea gives countries exclusive economic zones (EEZs) extending 200 nautical miles from their coast. Arctic nations (US, Russia, Canada, Norway, Denmark, Sweden, Finland) are now competing to claim extended continental shelves and resources. Russia has been militarising the Arctic since 2007; the US largely ignored it until recently.

## Multiple Perspectives
**US/NATO view:** Russia is a threat; we need bases to deter aggression and protect allies. China's Arctic ambitions are expansionist. We must secure our interests before it's too late.

**Russian perspective:** The Arctic is our backyard. NATO expansion there is hostile. We have legitimate security interests and historical claims.

**Chinese argument:** We're a near-Arctic state with legitimate interests in polar trade routes and resources. We should have a voice in Arctic governance.

**Greenlandic position:** We want independence and development, but we're small (56,000 people). We need investment and security guarantees. Playing great powers against each other is risky but necessary.

## Future Implications
If the US establishes bases, it escalates Arctic militarisation. Russia will respond with more military buildup. China will deepen Arctic investments. This could lead to an Arctic arms race or, conversely, a new Cold War flashpoint. Climate change will accelerate these dynamics as more resources become accessible.
