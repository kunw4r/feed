3:T849,## Historical Context
Tech regulation has been minimal in the US compared to Europe (GDPR, Digital Services Act). Trump's first presidency opposed regulation; Biden's administration pushed for more oversight. The 2016 election misinformation and ongoing concerns about AI safety have driven calls for regulation. China's tech dominance in some areas has made US policymakers anxious about competitiveness.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Trump Administration/Tech Industry**: Regulation kills innovation. American tech companies need freedom to compete globally. Self-regulation and market competition are more efficient than government rules. Deregulation will create jobs and keep the US ahead technologically.

**Regulators/Safety Advocates**: Platforms amplify misinformation and extremism. AI systems can discriminate and cause harm. Children are exploited on unregulated platforms. Without rules, companies prioritise profit over safety. Deregulation will worsen these problems.

**International Governments**: The EU and others have strict tech regulations. If the US deregulates, it creates a patchwork where companies follow different rules in different regions, complicating compliance and potentially allowing harmful content to spread globally.

**Researchers/Ethicists**: AI systems need safety testing before deployment. Unregulated AI could cause discrimination, spread misinformation at scale, and create unpredictable risks.

## Future Implications
If deregulation proceeds: more misinformation on platforms, potential increase in child exploitation, AI systems deployed without safety testing, but potentially faster innovation. If Congress blocks it: continued regulatory uncertainty and slower innovation. Likely outcome: partial deregulation with some safeguards remaining.

## What to Watch
- Congressional action and whether Democrats can block deregulation
- Tech company behaviour changes (will they moderate less?)
- Misinformation and hate speech trends on platforms
- International responses (EU may impose stricter rules, creating divergence)
