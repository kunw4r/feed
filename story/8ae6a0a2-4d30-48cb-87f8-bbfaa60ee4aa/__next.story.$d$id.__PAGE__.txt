Canada and the US signed the North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) in 1994, which eliminated most tariffs and integrated their economies. This created enormous benefits for both countries but also deep interdependence. In 2020, NAFTA was replaced with USMCA (United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement), which maintained most free trade benefits but added new rules on labour, environment, and digital trade. The current tensions reflect US concerns that it's not getting enough from the relationship.

## Multiple Perspectives
**US Government**: Canada isn't spending enough on defence (NATO recommends 2% of GDP); Canada's border is too porous for Chinese espionage; tariffs are justified leverage.
**Canadian Government**: Canada meets NATO commitments; defence spending increases are planned; tariffs would damage both economies; this is economic coercion.
**Canadian Business**: Tariffs would devastate supply chains; prices would rise; some manufacturing would relocate to the US or elsewhere; this is counterproductive.
**US Business**: Some support tariffs (to protect domestic industries); others oppose them (integrated supply chains benefit from free trade).
**Economists**: Tariffs would reduce GDP growth in both countries; consumers would pay higher prices; long-term damage outweighs any short-term gains.

This could fragment Western alliances if countries feel coerced rather than cooperating voluntarily.
