11:T404,Gaza, a densely populated territory of 2.3 million people, is experiencing what the UN calls a "humanitarian catastrophe" in February 2026. Following years of conflict and blockade, the territory faces severe shortages of food, clean water, medicine, and electricity. About 80% of the population needs humanitarian aid just to survive. Hospitals are barely functioning, malnutrition rates among children have doubled, and diseases like cholera are spreading due to poor sanitation. The blockade—imposed by Israel and Egypt for security reasons—means goods struggle to enter. International aid organisations say the situation is the worst in decades. The UN has warned of famine conditions. Egypt controls the southern border and has kept it mostly closed; Israel controls the northern border and limits what enters. Both cite security concerns about weapons smuggling, but aid groups say the restrictions are collective punishment of civilians. The crisis has triggered global protests and calls for emergency aid corridors.12:T67c,## Historical Context
Gaza has been under blockade since 2007 when Hamas took control. The 2023-2024 conflict devastated infrastructure further. Gaza is one of the most densely populated places on Earth (5,000+ people per square kilometre) with limited resources. It has no airport, no seaport under Palestinian control, and depends entirely on imports for survival.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Israel & Egypt**: The blockade prevents weapons and explosives reaching militant groups; they argue it's a security necessity.
**Palestinians & aid groups**: The blockade punishes 2.3 million civilians, including children and elderly, for actions of armed groups. They call it collective punishment (illegal under international law).
**International community**: Most acknowledge humanitarian crisis is real and unsustainable; disagree on how to solve it without compromising security.
**Aid organisations**: Say blockade is preventing them from delivering life-saving supplies and should be lifted or significantly eased.

## Future Implications
- Continued disease outbreaks and malnutrition could create public health emergency
- Desperation could fuel recruitment to militant groups
- International pressure on Israel and Egypt to ease restrictions
- Potential for renewed large-scale conflict if conditions worsen
- Long-term trauma and instability affecting entire region
