3:T624,The Australian Parliament has passed the Digital Platforms Accountability Act, requiring social media companies to verify users' ages, remove harmful content within 24 hours, and pay news organisations for content shared on their platforms. The legislation applies to all social media services with more than one million Australian users, including TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube. Companies that fail to comply face fines up to $50 million or 10% of their annual revenue, whichever is larger.

The law requires social media platforms to implement age verification systems to prevent children under 14 from accessing certain features, though users can appeal age restrictions. Platforms must also establish transparent processes for removing content that violates community standards, with appeals available for users who believe their content was wrongly removed. The "news media bargaining code" section requires platforms to negotiate payment with Australian news organisations for content that appears in news feeds, similar to regulations already implemented in the European Union.

Tech companies have expressed concerns about implementation costs and the challenge of complying with different regulations across countries. However, child safety advocates and news organisations have praised the legislation as necessary protection against harmful content and exploitation. The law takes effect in six months, giving companies time to implement compliance systems. ## Multiple Perspectives

News organisations globally may demand similar payment arrangements, fundamentally changing how social media platforms operate. Smaller platforms may struggle with compliance costs, potentially reducing competition and innovation.
