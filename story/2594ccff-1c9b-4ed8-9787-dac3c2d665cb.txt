11:T5db,## Historical Context
The Pacific has long been a Western sphere of influence, but China's Belt and Road Initiative (launched 2013) systematically built relationships through loans and projects. Traditional donors (Australia, US, NZ) focused on aid rather than infrastructure investment, leaving a gap China filled. Island nations face existential climate threats—Tuvalu and Kiribati could be uninhabitable within 50 years.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Pacific Island view**: China offers immediate economic development; Western climate pledges are slow and insufficient. Accepting Chinese loans is pragmatic survival.
**Australian/US view**: Chinese debt creates strategic vulnerability; Beijing could demand military access or political concessions if nations default.
**Chinese view**: Belt and Road is mutual development; Western criticism reflects anxiety about losing regional dominance.

## Future Implications
If Pacific Islands default on Chinese loans, Beijing could acquire strategic assets (ports, airfields). This would shift the balance of power in the Indo-Pacific, potentially threatening Australian and US defence interests. Climate migration could force hundreds of thousands of islanders to relocate—likely to Australia and NZ, creating immigration pressures.
