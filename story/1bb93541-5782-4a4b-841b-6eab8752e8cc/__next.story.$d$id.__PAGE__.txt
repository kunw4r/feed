3:T6b9,## Historical Context
The West Bank has been under Israeli military control since the 1967 Six-Day War. International law (UN Security Council Resolution 242) says Israel shouldn't acquire territory by force, yet Israeli settlements have grown there for decades. Previous peace attempts (Oslo Accords 1993, Camp David 2000) failed partly because of settlement expansion. Annexation would be the most dramatic Israeli claim to the territory since 1967.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Israeli government**: Settlements are necessary for security and represent historical Jewish connection to the land. Annexation formalises reality on the ground.
**Palestinians**: Annexation is theft of their homeland and makes a Palestinian state impossible—settlements already fragment Palestinian territory into disconnected areas.
**International community**: Most view annexation as violating international law and the principle that borders shouldn't change by force.
**US position**: Historically supports Israel but also backs a two-state solution; caught between allies.

## Future Implications
Annexation could trigger:
- Renewed Palestinian armed resistance and violence
- International sanctions against Israel
- Collapse of remaining peace negotiations
- Regional instability affecting neighbouring countries
- Shift in US-Israel relations depending on US administration response

## What to Watch
- Whether the US imposes consequences or remains silent
- Palestinian response: will they escalate violence or pursue international legal action?
- Arab states' reaction: will they cut ties with Israel or maintain recent normalisation agreements?
