3:T736,## Historical Context
Australia's inflation crisis began in 2021-2022 when supply chain disruptions and government stimulus drove prices up 11.7% by mid-2022—the highest in 32 years. The RBA responded aggressively, raising rates from 0.1% to 4.35% by late 2023. This was designed to cool demand and bring inflation back to the 2-3% target band. By February 2026, inflation has fallen to 3.2%, but wage growth (4.1% annually) remains above the RBA's comfort zone.

## Multiple Perspectives
**RBA's view**: Cutting rates too quickly risks reigniting inflation through wage-price spirals. Workers demand higher wages → employers raise prices → inflation returns → RBA must raise rates again. Better to move cautiously.

**Workers/renters**: The RBA is protecting bank profits while ordinary Australians suffer. Real wages (adjusted for inflation) are still negative—people are worse off than 2021 despite nominal pay rises.

**Business**: Uncertainty about rate timing makes investment planning difficult. Some want cuts to boost consumer spending; others fear inflation returning.

## Future Implications
If the RBA cuts rates in mid-2026, mortgage holders could save $100-200/month per $500k borrowed. But if inflation resurges, rates could spike again, creating volatility. The 2026 federal election (due by May 2026) will be fought partly on cost-of-living management—whichever party wins will face pressure to deliver relief quickly.

## What to Watch
- Wage growth data (monthly): If it stays above 4%, RBA stays patient
- CPI inflation (quarterly): Target is 2-3%; any move above 3.5% delays cuts
- Federal election outcome: Labor or Coalition will shape fiscal policy (tax cuts, welfare) alongside RBA decisions
