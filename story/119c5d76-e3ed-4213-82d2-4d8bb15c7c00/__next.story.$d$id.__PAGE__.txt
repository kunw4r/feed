Large language models (LLMs) emerged from decades of machine learning research, but the transformer architecture introduced in 2017 enabled the rapid scaling that led to ChatGPT in 2022. Each generation since has roughly doubled in capability while requiring exponentially more computing power and training data. GPT-5 represents the fifth major iteration of OpenAI's flagship model, following GPT-3 (2020), GPT-3.5 (2022), and GPT-4 (2023).

## Multiple Perspectives
Proponents argue that more capable AI systems will accelerate scientific discovery, improve healthcare diagnostics, and automate dangerous or tedious work. Critics counter that we lack adequate regulatory frameworks to manage risks, and that concentrating such powerful technology in a few companies creates dangerous power imbalances. Some researchers worry about "capability overhang"—the possibility that AI systems possess dangerous abilities we haven't yet discovered.

## Future Implications
If GPT-5 delivers on its claims, it may accelerate AI adoption across professional services, potentially displacing white-collar workers faster than predicted. The model's improved reasoning could enable autonomous AI agents that operate with minimal human oversight, raising governance questions. Competition may force other companies to release increasingly powerful systems on shorter timelines, potentially bypassing safety testing.
