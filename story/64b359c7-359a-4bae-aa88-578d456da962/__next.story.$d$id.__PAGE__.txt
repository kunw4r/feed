3:T4b6,Europe is experiencing a new wave of migration pressure, with over 200,000 people attempting to cross borders in the past month alone. Most are fleeing conflict in Syria, Afghanistan, and Somalia, or seeking economic opportunity. In response, several EU countries are building or reinforcing border walls: Hungary, Poland, and now Italy are constructing barriers. The EU passed new asylum rules this month that make it harder to claim refugee status—applicants must prove they face individual persecution, not just come from dangerous countries. This matters because it reflects a fundamental tension: the EU's founding principle is free movement within borders, but member states increasingly want to control who enters. The rules also shift responsibility to border countries (Greece, Italy) which bear the burden of processing migrants. Human rights groups warn the new rules violate international refugee conventions. Meanwhile, migration is becoming a weapon: Russia is allegedly encouraging migration to Europe to destabilise the EU; Turkey controls migration flows as leverage in negotiations. The next weeks will show whether Europe can manage migration humanely or whether it becomes a fortress.4:T875,## Historical Context
Post-WWII, Europe built the refugee convention (1951) after the Holocaust, committing to protect people fleeing persecution. The EU's Schengen Area (1985) abolished internal borders, allowing free movement. This worked when migration was low. The 2015 crisis (1.3 million arrivals) overwhelmed systems and sparked backlash. Now, 2026 sees renewed pressure from Syria (ongoing civil war), Afghanistan (Taliban rule), and climate-driven migration from Africa. Unlike 2015, European publics are less sympathetic, and far-right parties (see story 1) have made immigration a central issue.

## Multiple Perspectives
**Pro-migration advocates argue:** Refugees are fleeing real dangers; we have legal and moral obligations to help. Migration enriches societies economically and culturally. Walls don't work—they just push people toward dangerous routes.

**Border-control advocates claim:** Europe can't absorb unlimited migration; it strains housing, jobs, and public services. We must distinguish between refugees (fleeing persecution) and economic migrants. Tough borders deter dangerous journeys.

**Border countries (Greece, Italy) complain:** We're overwhelmed. Richer EU countries should share responsibility, not just dump migrants on us. We need EU funding and support.

**Migrants' perspective:** We're fleeing death and poverty. Borders are arbitrary; we deserve a chance at safety and dignity.

## Future Implications
If the EU continues tightening rules, more migrants will use dangerous routes (boats, smugglers), leading to more deaths. If rules are loosened, far-right parties will gain support. The EU is caught between humanitarian values and political reality. This could lead to a two-tier system: some countries accept migrants, others reject them, fracturing EU unity.
