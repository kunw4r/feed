North Korea has pursued nuclear weapons since the 1980s. In 2006, it conducted its first nuclear test. Since then, it's tested bombs multiple times and developed missiles of increasing range. The 2015 Iran nuclear deal (which the US later abandoned) showed North Korea that negotiating away nuclear weapons is risky—Iran gave up its program and the US withdrew anyway. This convinced North Korea never to disarm.

## Multiple Perspectives
**US/South Korea/Japan view**: North Korea's weapons are destabilising and threaten regional security. They want sanctions and diplomacy to force disarmament.

**North Korea's view**: Nuclear weapons are survival insurance. Without them, the US could invade (as it did in Iraq and Afghanistan). The weapons prove North Korea is powerful and deserves respect.

**China's view**: North Korea is a buffer state between China and US-allied South Korea. China doesn't want regime collapse or US troops on its border, so it quietly supports North Korea despite public criticism.

**Russia's view**: Russia sees North Korea as a useful counterweight to US power in Asia. Russia and North Korea have strengthened ties since 2022.

## Future Implications
North Korea will likely continue testing missiles. Each test makes weapons more reliable. Eventually, North Korea might sell missile technology to other countries (it already sells weapons to Russia and Iran). This spreads nuclear risk globally. A miscalculation during a crisis could trigger war on the Korean Peninsula, affecting millions of people and global supply chains.
