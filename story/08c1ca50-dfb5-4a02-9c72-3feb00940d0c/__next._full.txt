11:T54f,Sudan's two-year civil war has created what the UN is calling the world's worst humanitarian crisis, with over 10 million people displaced and famine conditions spreading across multiple regions. The conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces militia has destroyed hospitals, schools, and food supply chains, leaving civilians without access to basic necessities. Aid organisations estimate that 25 million Sudanese—nearly half the population—need emergency assistance, but fighting makes it nearly impossible to deliver food and medicine safely.

The crisis matters globally because Sudan sits at the crossroads of Africa, the Middle East, and the Mediterranean. When millions of people face starvation, they often flee as refugees, affecting neighbouring countries like Egypt, South Sudan, and Ethiopia. The instability also disrupts global grain supplies—Sudan was once a major food exporter—and creates power vacuums that extremist groups exploit.

International donors have pledged billions in aid, but most funds never reach people on the ground due to insecurity and corruption. The UN estimates it needs $2.6 billion just to provide emergency relief through 2026. Sudan has experienced conflict since independence in 1956, but the 2023 war between General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan's military and the Rapid Support Forces (a paramilitary group) represents a complete state collapse. The RSF emerged from the Janjaweed militias that committed genocide in Darfur (2003-2009). This isn't a simple political dispute—it's a fight for control of state resources, gold mines, and military power.

## Multiple Perspectives
**International view**: The UN and Western governments see this as a humanitarian emergency requiring urgent intervention and ceasefire negotiations. **Regional view**: Egypt and Saudi Arabia have competing interests—Egypt fears instability on its border; Saudi Arabia backs the military faction. **Local view**: Sudanese civilians are caught between two forces that both commit atrocities; many have lost everything and see no end in sight.

## Future Implications
If the war continues, Sudan could fragment into failed states controlled by warlords. The refugee crisis could destabilise Egypt (already hosting 6 million refugees) and create pressure on Europe through Libya. Climate change is making the situation worse—drought is reducing available farmland, intensifying competition for resources.
