This winter is the first in 30 years that Europe isn't reliant on Russian energy supplies—a major geopolitical victory but one that cost €200 billion in infrastructure investment.

Why this matters: For decades, Russia supplied 40% of Europe's natural gas, giving it enormous political leverage. The EU chose independence, building LNG terminals and diversifying suppliers. This reduced Russia's revenue by €50 billion annually, weakening its ability to fund the war.

After the Soviet Union collapsed in 1991, Russia and Europe developed deep energy ties. Russian gas was cheap and reliable; Europe needed it for heating and electricity. By 2021, Russia supplied 40% of EU gas. This interdependence was meant to create peace through economic ties—the logic being nations won't war if they're economically linked. Russia's invasion proved this wrong. Europe realised energy dependence was a strategic vulnerability. The EU then pursued a rapid transition to LNG and renewables.

## Multiple Perspectives
**EU perspective**: Energy independence is essential for security and sovereignty. The transition, though costly, is worth the price of freedom from Russian coercion. Renewable energy also addresses climate change.

**Russian perspective**: Europe's shift is hostile and economically damaging. Russia views energy exports as legitimate leverage and sees the EU's diversification as Western aggression against Russian interests.

**Australian perspective**: LNG exports to Europe have become highly profitable. Australian companies (Woodside, Santos, Shell) are earning record revenues. However, this diverts gas from Asian markets and raises domestic prices in Australia.

**Climate advocates**: While renewable energy is good, LNG is still fossil fuel and produces emissions. True energy independence requires faster transition to wind, solar, and nuclear.

**Energy-poor households**: Higher energy prices strain budgets. Many Europeans face energy poverty (inability to afford adequate heating/cooling).

## Future Implications
If Europe maintains LNG imports: Russia loses long-term revenue and leverage. Europe becomes more resilient to energy blackmail. If Europe accelerates renewables: LNG demand drops, and Australian exports decline. If Russia develops alternative markets (China, India): It mitigates revenue losses but at lower prices.
